# Automatic Cars — Hydraulic Torque Converter (Drehmomentwandler)

---

## How the Hydraulic Converter Works

The hydraulic torque converter **replaces the clutch** found in manual cars. It uses hydraulic fluid to transfer engine power to the transmission — there is no direct mechanical connection between engine and wheels at low speeds, only a fluid coupling.

This means:
- No clutch pedal
- The engine keeps running when the car is stationary
- Gear changes happen automatically based on speed and load
- The engine can never be overwhelmed by a sudden load — the fluid absorbs any mismatch

---

## Key Driving Characteristics

**Creep effect (Kriecheffekt)**
When in Drive (D) with your foot off the brake, the car moves forward slowly on its own. The converter transmits a small amount of torque even at idle. You must hold the footbrake when stationary.

**Reduced engine braking**
The converter allows slip between engine and wheels, so lifting off the accelerator provides much less engine braking than in a manual. You must rely more on the **footbrake**, especially on downhill gradients — and plan braking earlier.

**Kickdown**
Pressing the accelerator fully to the floor triggers a forced downshift for maximum acceleration. Useful for overtaking.

---

## Selector Lever (Wählhebel)

The selector lever is the gear shifter in an automatic car — the lever you move between P, R, N, D. It is called a "selector" because you are selecting a mode, not manually choosing a gear ratio.

| Position | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **P** | Park — mechanically locks the transmission. Always also use handbrake on slopes. |
| **R** | Reverse |
| **N** | Neutral — converter disengaged |
| **D** | Drive — normal forward driving |

---

## Rules for Using the Selector Lever

**When selecting a gear, hold the car with the service brake (footbrake).**
The creep effect means the car moves immediately when D or R is engaged. Most automatics also have a shift lock — the brake must be pressed before the lever will move out of P.

**The engine must NOT be switched off to move the selector.**
The engine must be running to drive. What matters is that the **car is stationary** (not moving) before shifting into R or P — not that the engine is off. These are two different things:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Car stationary** | Vehicle not moving — engine still running |
| **Engine stopped** | Ignition off — car completely dead |

Shifting into R or P while the car is still rolling at speed can cause serious transmission damage.

---

## What is Stalling? (And Why It Doesn't Apply Here)

**Stalling** means the engine suddenly cuts out and dies while the car is in use.

In a manual car, the engine is connected to the wheels through a clutch — a mechanical plate engaged or disengaged by the clutch pedal. When you release the clutch, you ask the engine to suddenly take on the load of moving the car. If the engine is not spinning fast enough, it cannot handle that load and stops completely. That is a stall.

**The manual habit — raising revs when moving off:**
In a manual, moving off from a standstill requires:
1. Press clutch pedal fully
2. Select first gear
3. Press accelerator slightly — raising engine speed to ~1,500–2,000 rpm
4. Slowly release the clutch until the biting point
5. Release clutch fully while maintaining revs

Without step 3, the engine has no spare power to absorb the sudden load and stalls. New drivers stall constantly by releasing the clutch too quickly or without enough revs.

**In an automatic, this does not apply.**
The hydraulic converter transmits power gradually through fluid — there is no sudden mechanical engagement. When D is selected, the engine pushes fluid smoothly with no shock load. Stalling is essentially impossible in normal automatic operation, so raising revs before selecting a gear serves no purpose.

## Exam-Relevant Summary

| Statement | Correct? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Hold car with service brake when selecting a gear | Yes | Creep effect + shift lock |
| Selector must only be moved when engine has stopped | No | Engine must be running; car should be stationary, not engine off |
| Must raise engine revs to prevent stalling | No | No clutch, no stall risk — hydraulic converter absorbs load smoothly |

**License restriction:** If you pass your driving test in an automatic, your license carries code **78**, restricting you to automatics only. To drive a manual, you must re-test in one.
