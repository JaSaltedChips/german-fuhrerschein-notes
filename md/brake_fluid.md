# Brake Fluid (Bremsflüssigkeit)

## Why Regular Brake Fluid Changes Are Required

Brake fluid is **hygroscopic** — it absorbs moisture from the atmosphere over time. As water content rises, three problems develop:

1. **Component damage** — water causes corrosion of metal parts (calipers, cylinders, brake lines) and degrades rubber seals
2. **Boiling point drops** — fresh fluid boils at ~250°C; water-contaminated fluid can boil as low as ~140°C
3. **Brake failure under heavy load** — if fluid boils during hard braking, vapour lock occurs

## Vapour Lock

**Vapour lock = brake failure, not strong braking.**

| State | Compressibility | Effect when pedal pressed |
|---|---|---|
| Normal fluid (liquid) | Incompressible | Pressure transmits fully to calipers → strong braking |
| Vapour bubbles in fluid | Compressible | Pedal compresses the bubbles → pressure never reaches calipers → brakes fail |

When vapour lock occurs, the brake pedal travels to the floor with little or no resistance — the system has effectively failed.

## The Chain of Events

> Water accumulates in fluid → boiling point drops → heavy braking generates heat → fluid boils → vapour bubbles form → vapour lock → brake failure + corrosion of components

## Key Principle

Brakes work because liquids are incompressible — pressure at the pedal transmits instantly and fully to the wheels. Gas in the system destroys this incompressibility and causes failure.

Regular brake fluid changes keep water content within safe limits and maintain a high boiling point.
