# Engine Wear, Cold Starts, and RPM

## Why Short Journeys with a Cold Engine Cause More Wear

1. **Poor oil lubrication** — cold oil is thick and slow to circulate; moving parts run without sufficient lubrication initially
2. **Wrong component clearances** — metal parts haven't thermally expanded to their designed tolerances
3. **Fuel dilutes the oil** — cold engines run rich; excess fuel washes cylinder walls and mixes into the oil
4. **Water condensation** — combustion produces water vapour which condenses inside a cold engine, causing corrosion

On a longer journey the engine reaches operating temperature (~5–10 min) and all four problems resolve. On a short journey the engine is switched off before this ever happens.

## Correct Warm-Up Approach

- **Do not idle to warm up** — idling is slow, inefficient, and illegal in Germany (§30 StVO prohibits unnecessary idling)
- **Drive away immediately but gently** — the engine warms up faster under gentle load than at idle
- Avoid hard acceleration, high RPM, and high speeds until the temperature gauge reaches normal

## RPM, Gear, and Speed

At any given speed, gear choice determines RPM:
- Higher gear → lower RPM (for same speed)
- Lower gear → higher RPM (for same speed)

"Keep RPM low" means **shift up to an appropriate gear for your current speed** — not that you need to drive faster.

Optimal RPM range for most petrol engines during normal driving: **~1,500–3,000 RPM**

## Engine Lugging

Lugging = being in too high a gear for your speed, causing RPM to drop very low under load.

**Why it is harmful:**

| Cause | Effect |
|---|---|
| Irregular combustion at low RPM | Engine knock — damages pistons, bearings, cylinder head |
| Oil pump runs slowly at low RPM | Reduced oil pressure and lubrication |
| Engine producing maximum torque to recover | Stress on crankshaft, connecting rods, pistons |
| Incomplete combustion | Carbon deposits on valves and pistons |
| Inefficient operation | Can worsen fuel economy despite high gear |

## The Balance

Both extremes cause damage:
- **Too high RPM** — over-revving, heat, mechanical stress
- **Too low RPM** — lugging, knock, poor lubrication

Goal: stay within the efficient RPM range at whatever speed you are travelling, using an appropriate gear.
