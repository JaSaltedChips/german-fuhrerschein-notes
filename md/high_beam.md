# High Beam (Fernlicht) & High Beam Assist (Fernlichtassistent)

## Overview

High beam and high beam assist are two different things that are often confused. This document covers both — what they are, why they exist, and the rules governing their use.

---

## High Beam (Fernlicht)

### What It Is

High beam is the bright headlight setting that illuminates significantly further ahead than dipped headlights:

| Headlight setting | Illumination range |
|---|---|
| Dipped headlights (Abblendlicht) | ~40–60 metres |
| High beam (Fernlicht) | ~100–150 metres or more |

### Why It Exists

On a dark, unlit rural road at 100 km/h, the emergency stopping distance is approximately **80 metres**. Dipped headlights only illuminate 40–60 metres ahead — meaning you are effectively driving faster than your headlights can see. An obstacle at the edge of your visibility would give you no time to stop.

High beam extends your visibility to 100–150 metres, giving you sufficient distance to react and stop safely. Without it, driving at speed on completely dark roads would be genuinely dangerous.

> High beam exists because dipped headlights alone are insufficient for safe driving at speed on dark, unlit roads.

### When You Are Allowed to Use It

All of the following conditions must be true simultaneously:

- You are **outside a built-up area** on an **unlit road**
- There is **no oncoming traffic** visible
- You are **not following closely** behind another vehicle
- There is **no fog, rain, or snow**

If any one of these conditions is not met, high beam must not be used.

### When You Must Switch Back to Dipped Headlights

You must switch back immediately when:

- An **oncoming vehicle** becomes visible (at approximately 200 metres or earlier)
- You are **following another vehicle** — high beam dazzles the driver ahead via their mirrors
- You **enter a built-up area** — street lighting is generally sufficient
- **Fog, rain, or snow** is present — high beam reflects back and actively reduces visibility

---

## High Beam Assist (Fernlichtassistent)

### What It Is

High beam assist is a **driver assistance system** — not the headlight itself. It uses a forward-facing camera to detect oncoming vehicles and vehicles ahead, then automatically switches between high beam and dipped headlights without the driver having to act manually.

It applies exactly the same rules as manual high beam use — the difference is purely in execution: the system handles the switching so the driver does not forget or react too slowly.

### Why It Is Useful

Manually switching between high beam and dipped headlights requires constant attention. Drivers often forget to switch, switch too late, or do not bother switching at all. High beam assist removes this burden and ensures the correct headlight setting is always active, reducing the risk of dazzling other road users.

---

## Key Distinction

| | High beam (Fernlicht) | High beam assist (Fernlichtassistent) |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | The bright headlight setting itself | A camera-based automatic switching system |
| Why it exists | Safe visibility at speed on dark unlit roads | Automates the switching between high and dipped |
| When to use | Dark unlit road, no other traffic, no bad weather | Activated by the driver — system handles the rest |
| Restricted because | Dazzles oncoming drivers and those ahead | Avoids dazzling by switching automatically |

---

## Summary Rule

> Use high beam on **dark, unlit roads outside built-up areas** when there is **no other traffic** and **no adverse weather**. Switch to dipped headlights the **moment** oncoming traffic or a vehicle ahead is visible.
