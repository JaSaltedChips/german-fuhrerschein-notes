# Hybrid Vehicles — HEV vs PHEV

## Regular Hybrid (HEV — Hybrid Electric Vehicle)

- Has both a combustion engine and an electric motor
- Battery charges **only** through recuperation and the combustion engine generating electricity
- **Cannot be plugged in** — no external charging
- Battery is small
- Electric-only range is minimal (often just a few km, mainly assists the engine rather than replacing it)
- The combustion engine always plays the primary role

## Plug-in Hybrid (PHEV — Plug-in Hybrid Electric Vehicle)

- Also has both a combustion engine and an electric motor
- Battery charges through recuperation, the engine, **and external mains supply**
- **Can be plugged in** — the defining difference
- Battery is significantly larger
- Electric-only range is meaningful — typically 30–80 km depending on the model
- Can run purely on electricity for daily short trips; combustion engine kicks in when battery is depleted or on longer journeys

## Comparison

| Feature | Regular Hybrid (HEV) | Plug-in Hybrid (PHEV) |
|---|---|---|
| External charging | No | Yes |
| Battery size | Small | Large |
| Electric-only range | Very limited | 30–80 km typically |
| Primary power source | Combustion engine | Electric (if charged) |

## Optimising Fuel Consumption in a PHEV

1. **Charge from mains supply regularly** — the most effective method; starts each journey with a full battery
2. **Use electric drive through driving style** — smooth, anticipatory driving at moderate speeds keeps the car in electric mode longer
3. Recuperation alone is **not sufficient** — relying only on regenerative braking never fully charges the battery and defeats the purpose of the plug-in capability

> A PHEV used without ever plugging in essentially behaves like a regular hybrid, wasting its larger battery and the fuel savings it was designed to deliver.

## Alternative Fuels (Alternativkraftstoffe)

Any fuel other than conventional petrol (Benzin) or diesel:

| Type | Examples | Emission |
|---|---|---|
| Electric (BEV) | Battery electric vehicles | Zero tailpipe emissions |
| Hydrogen (FCEV) | Fuel cell vehicles | Water vapour only |
| Gas-based | LPG (Autogas), CNG (Erdgas), LNG | Lower than petrol/diesel |
| Biofuels | Ethanol (E10, E85), Biodiesel (B7) | Partially renewable |
| Synthetic (E-Fuels) | Produced using renewable electricity | Potentially carbon-neutral |

> Alternative fuels do **not** limit vehicle speed or performance — electric vehicles in particular can be very fast.
