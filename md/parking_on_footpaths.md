# Parking on Footpaths (Parken auf Gehwegen)

## Overview

In Germany, parking on footpaths is generally prohibited. However, under §12 StVO, an exception exists where footpaths have been **specially designated** for parking. Even then, strict conditions apply — including a limit on the permissible total mass of the vehicle.

---

## The Mass Limit

Only vehicles with a permissible total mass (*zulässige Gesamtmasse*) of **2.8 tonnes (2,800 kg) or less** may park on a designated footpath. This effectively restricts footpath parking to passenger cars and light vehicles. Heavier vehicles — vans, trucks, and larger commercial vehicles — are excluded regardless of whether the footpath is designated.

The reason for this limit is structural: footpaths are not built to bear the weight of heavy vehicles and would be damaged by repeated use from anything heavier.

---

## Additional Conditions

The mass limit alone is not sufficient. All three of the following conditions must be satisfied simultaneously:

### 1. Specially Designated Footpath
The footpath must be marked with the appropriate signage indicating that parking is permitted. Without this designation, parking on the footpath is prohibited outright, regardless of the vehicle's mass.

### 2. Pedestrian Clearance of at Least 1.5 Metres
Even when parking is permitted, a clear and unobstructed passage of **at least 1.5 metres** must remain on the footpath for pedestrians at all times. A vehicle parked in a way that blocks pedestrian access — even partially — is in violation, even if the footpath is designated and the vehicle is within the mass limit.

### 3. Vehicle Mass Within Limit
The vehicle's permissible total mass must not exceed **2.8 tonnes**.

---

## Summary

| Condition | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Footpath designation | Must be specially marked for parking |
| Permissible total mass | Maximum **2.8 tonnes (2,800 kg)** |
| Pedestrian clearance | At least **1.5 metres** must remain free |

All three conditions must be met. Satisfying only one or two is not sufficient — parking would still be considered unlawful.

---

## Key Takeaway

Footpath parking is the exception, not the rule. The designated sign grants permission in principle, but the vehicle's mass and the remaining space for pedestrians determine whether that permission actually applies in a given situation.
