# Recuperation & Inhibited Braking (EV)

> **Exam relevance:** This is a prominent technical concept featured on the official German driver's licence theory exam *(Führerscheinprüfung)*.

When a driving context mentions that recuperation can **"inhibit" braking action**, it means the car's automatic electric deceleration (regenerative braking) is weakened or completely unavailable — forcing you to rely solely on the physical brake pads.

---

## How Recuperation Works (Normal Conditions)

When you lift your foot off the accelerator or lightly press the brake pedal, an EV reverses its electric motor to act as a generator. This slows the vehicle down while converting kinetic energy into electricity to recharge the battery.

The motor can only generate this stopping power if **the battery has room to safely store the incoming electricity**.

---

## The Two Triggers That Inhibit Recuperation

German traffic theory standards define two primary conditions that cause the vehicle's management software to restrict or disable recuperation:

| Trigger | Why it inhibits recuperation |
|---|---|
| **Fully charged battery (100% SoC)** | No storage room left — the system cannot push more current into a full battery, so recuperative braking is temporarily turned off. |
| **Low operating temperature** | Lithium-ion batteries cannot safely accept high charging currents when cold. In freezing conditions before the battery warms up, the system deactivates or heavily throttles recuperation. |

---

## What This Means for the Driver

### Changed pedal feel
When recuperation is inhibited, the car will **not slow down automatically** when you lift your foot off the accelerator — the "one-pedal driving" effect is lost.

### Heavier brake application required
To achieve the same stopping distance, you must press the physical brake pedal **much harder than usual**. The mechanical friction brakes have to do 100% of the work alone.

---

## Secondary Risk: Rust & Brake Fade

Because EVs rely heavily on recuperation in everyday driving, the physical brake pads and discs are rarely used. Over time this causes:

- **Rust and road grit** to build up on the steel brake discs
- A temporarily **reduced mechanical friction** surface if an emergency stop is suddenly needed
- A slight **delay in reaching full stopping power** until the rust layer is worn off
