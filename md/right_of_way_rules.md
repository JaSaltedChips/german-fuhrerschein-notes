# Right-of-Way Rules — Germany (StVO)

---

## 1. Right-Before-Left Rule (Rechts vor Links)

At unmarked intersections with no signs, traffic lights, or priority markings, the vehicle coming from the **right** has priority.

### When It Applies
- Uncontrolled intersections with no signs
- Equal-ranked roads meeting

### When It Does NOT Apply
- A priority road (Vorfahrtsstraße) is present — signs override Rechts vor Links
- Traffic lights are present
- A Stop or Give-way sign is present

### Key Point
If you are unsure whether you are on a priority road, assume Rechts vor Links applies.

---

## 2. Left-Turn Rule (Linksabbiegen)

When turning left at an intersection, you must yield to oncoming traffic. The specific behaviour depends on what the oncoming vehicle intends to do.

### Scenarios

#### a) Oncoming vehicle turns right
Both vehicles end up on the same road. Paths converge rather than directly cross.
- If the road is wide enough, you can often proceed simultaneously — the right-turner takes the short path near the kerb, you take the longer arc
- If the road is narrow or paths conflict, yield to the right-turning vehicle first
- They are still oncoming traffic and have priority over you

#### b) Oncoming vehicle goes straight
**Yield completely.** Oncoming traffic going straight has absolute priority over you turning left. Wait until they have fully cleared the intersection, then turn.

#### c) Both turning left — Linksabbieger-Begegnung
Neither yields to the other. The German rule is to pass **in front of each other** (voreinander) — both turn simultaneously, each crossing in front of the other.

If physically impossible (narrow road or markings indicate otherwise): pass **behind each other** (hintereinander).

### Summary

| Oncoming intention | Your behaviour |
|---|---|
| Turns right | Yield if paths conflict; can go simultaneously if road is wide enough |
| Goes straight | Yield completely — absolute priority |
| Turns left | No yielding — pass in front of each other (voreinander) |

## 3. Turning Left on a Priority Road (Vorfahrtsstraße)

Being on a Vorfahrtsstraße gives you priority over cross traffic — but not unconditionally when turning left.

### Against side road traffic (cars from right and left)

You have priority. Vehicles on side roads have Stop or Give-way signs and must wait for you, regardless of your intention to turn left.

### Against oncoming traffic on the same Vorfahrtsstraße

You must still yield. Priority road status protects you from cross traffic — it does not override the left-turn obligation toward oncoming traffic on your own road. The standard left-turn rule applies.

### Summary

| Traffic | Your priority? |
|---|---|
| Cars from side roads (cross traffic) | **Yes** — they must wait |
| Oncoming vehicle on the Vorfahrtsstraße | **No** — yield before turning left |

**Key principle:** Priority road status protects you from cross traffic. It does not override the left-turn obligation toward oncoming traffic on your own road.

## 4. Stop Sign vs Give-Way Sign — Who Has Priority?

### The Signs

| Sign | Obligation |
|---|---|
| **Stop (Zeichen 206)** | Must come to a complete stop and yield to traffic on the priority road |
| **Give-way (Zeichen 205)** | Must yield to traffic on the priority road — no obligation to stop completely |

Both signs define your obligation toward the **priority road**, not your rank relative to other secondary road users at the same junction.

### Scenario: You have a Stop sign, car from the left has a Give-way sign

Between two secondary road users at the same junction, normal right-of-way rules apply. The car is coming from your **left** — you are to their **right**. **Rechts vor Links** applies: **you have priority** over the car from the left.

However, your Stop sign still legally requires you to **come to a complete stop** before proceeding. You stop, confirm it is safe, and then go. The car from the left must wait for you.

### Key Insight

The Stop and Give-way signs regulate your relationship with the **main road**. Once you have fulfilled that obligation, normal rules (Rechts vor Links) determine priority between secondary road users.
