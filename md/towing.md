# Towing Rules (Abschleppen & Anhänger) — Germany

## Overview

Towing in Germany covers two distinct situations, each governed by its own set of rules:

1. **Towing a trailer** (Anhänger ziehen) — a trailer attached to your vehicle as part of normal driving
2. **Towing a broken-down vehicle** (Abschleppen) — recovering another vehicle that cannot move under its own power

The rules differ significantly between the two. Understanding which situation applies is the first step.

---

## Part 1 — Towing a Trailer (Anhänger)

### Speed Limits

When towing a trailer, the normal speed limits no longer apply. Reduced limits apply everywhere:

| Location | Max speed |
|---|---|
| Inside built-up areas (innerorts) | 50 km/h (unchanged) |
| Outside built-up areas (außerorts) | **80 km/h** (reduced from 100) |
| Motorway (Autobahn) | **80 km/h** (reduced from 130) |

**Exception — the 100 km/h approval:** If the trailer has been officially tested and approved, it may carry a **"100" sticker**. In that case, the limit outside built-up areas and on motorways rises to **100 km/h**.

### Braking System

Trailers with a permissible total mass (*zulässige Gesamtmasse*) exceeding **750 kg** must be equipped with their own braking system. Lighter trailers do not require independent brakes.

### Driving Licence Requirements

| Situation | Licence required |
|---|---|
| Trailer up to 750 kg, or combined weight of car + trailer within 3,500 kg | **Class B** |
| Combined weight of car + trailer exceeds 3,500 kg | **Class BE** |

### Safety Requirements

- The trailer must be **properly coupled** to the towing vehicle with a safety chain or cable as a backup in case the main coupling fails
- The trailer must have its own **rear lights, brake lights, and indicators** — these must function correctly at all times
- The towing vehicle's **number plate** must be displayed at the rear of the trailer
- The driver must have adequate **rear visibility** — extended mirrors may be required if the trailer is wider than the towing vehicle

## Part 2 — Towing a Broken-Down Vehicle (Abschleppen)

### Speed Limit

The maximum permitted speed when towing a broken-down vehicle is **50 km/h** — no exceptions.

### Hazard Lights

Both the towing vehicle and the towed vehicle must have **hazard lights on** throughout the entire tow.

### Driver in the Towed Vehicle

The towed vehicle must have a driver inside to **steer and apply the brakes**. It cannot simply be dragged unmanned.

### Destination

You may only tow a broken-down vehicle to the **nearest garage or repair workshop**. Long-distance towing or towing to a preferred location further away is not permitted.

### Not Permitted on Motorways

Towing a broken-down vehicle on the motorway is **not permitted**. The vehicle must be recovered professionally — for example by the ADAC.

---

## Part 3 — The Connection Between Vehicles

### Type of Connection

Two types of connection are used when towing a broken-down vehicle:

**Tow rope or tow strap (Abschleppseil):**
A flexible connection. Because it goes slack and then pulls tight, the driver of the towed vehicle must pay close attention at all times — particularly when braking — to avoid running into the towing vehicle. Requires more skill and concentration.

**Tow bar (Abschleppstange):**
A rigid connection that maintains a fixed distance between the two vehicles. There is no slack, making it more predictable and safer than a rope. Preferred where possible.

### Maximum Distance

The connection must be a maximum of **5 metres** in length — whether using a rope or a bar.

### Visibility

If the connection is not clearly visible to other road users (for example, a thin rope or strap), it must be **clearly marked** — typically with a warning flag or brightly coloured cloth — so that following traffic understands the two vehicles are linked.

| | Tow rope | Tow bar |
|---|---|---|
| Flexibility | Flexible — goes slack | Rigid — fixed distance |
| Max length | **5 metres** | Fixed by bar design |
| Risk | Higher — requires attentive towed driver | Lower |
| Visibility marking | Required if not clearly visible | Less critical |

## Part 4 — Motorcycles: Special Restrictions

Motorcycles are subject to specific restrictions that set them apart from all other vehicles.

### Motorcycles Cannot Be Towed by Rope or Bar

A motorcycle cannot be towed using a conventional rope or bar. Because it balances on two wheels and requires an active rider to remain upright, a motorcycle being pulled along the road would simply fall over. Conventional towing is therefore not possible.

### Motorcycles Cannot Be the Towing Vehicle

Motorcycles are also not permitted to tow other broken-down vehicles. They lack the structural towing points, the stability, and the mass needed to safely pull another vehicle — especially under braking.

### How Motorcycles Must Be Recovered

When a motorcycle breaks down, it must be **loaded onto a recovery vehicle or trailer**. It cannot be towed along the road on its own two wheels.

| Scenario | Permitted? |
|---|---|
| Car towing a broken-down car | Yes — rope or bar, max 50 km/h, max 5m |
| Motorcycle towing a broken-down vehicle | **No** |
| Motorcycle being towed by rope or bar | **No** |
| Motorcycle being recovered | Must be **loaded onto a recovery vehicle or trailer** |

## Part 5 — Electric Vehicles: Special Restrictions

Electric vehicles (EVs) have a critical restriction that applies on top of all other towing rules.

### EVs Must Not Be Flat-Towed

An electric vehicle **must not be towed with all four wheels on the ground** — the conventional method used for broken-down combustion engine vehicles. When an EV's driven wheels rotate while being towed, the electric motor spins and acts as a **generator**, causing:

- Unintended charging or overloading of the battery management system
- Overheating of the electric motor
- Unexpected braking forces transmitted through the drivetrain
- Potential permanent damage to the drivetrain or battery

### What Must Happen Instead

A broken-down EV must be **loaded onto a flatbed recovery vehicle** with all wheels lifted completely off the road. It cannot be towed by rope or bar in the conventional sense.

### Accidents and High Voltage

After an accident involving an EV, emergency responders must be **warned of the high-voltage system**. The battery pack operates at several hundred volts and poses a serious electrocution risk if the vehicle structure is compromised.

### Battery Fires

EV battery fires are significantly harder to extinguish than conventional fires and can **reignite hours or even days** after appearing to be out. Firefighters must be informed immediately that the vehicle is electric so they can deploy the correct approach.

### Acoustic Warning System (AVAS)

Electric vehicles are nearly silent at low speeds, which poses a danger to pedestrians and cyclists who rely on engine noise as a warning. EU regulations require EVs to be fitted with an **Acoustic Vehicle Alerting System (AVAS)** — an artificial sound that activates automatically at speeds **below 20 km/h**.

## Complete Summary

| Rule | Trailer towing | Towing broken-down vehicle |
|---|---|---|
| Max speed (motorway) | **80 km/h** (or 100 with sticker) | **50 km/h** |
| Max speed (außerorts) | **80 km/h** (or 100 with sticker) | **50 km/h** |
| Hazard lights | Not required | **Both vehicles — mandatory** |
| Driver in towed vehicle | N/A | **Mandatory** |
| Permitted on motorway | Yes | **No** |
| Destination | Anywhere | **Nearest garage only** |
| Connection max length | N/A | **5 metres** |
| Motorcycles | Can tow trailer (with correct licence) | **Cannot tow or be towed — must be loaded onto flatbed** |
| Electric vehicles | Normal trailer rules apply | **Cannot be flat-towed — must be loaded onto flatbed** |
