# Trailer Parking Rules (Anhänger parken) — Germany

## Overview

Trailers are treated differently from ordinary vehicles in German traffic law because a detached trailer is not merely parked — it is effectively being stored on a public road. Unlike a car, a detached trailer has no driver, no engine, and no obligation to move. Left unchecked, it occupies public space indefinitely, causes visibility obstructions near junctions, and poses a safety risk if it rolls or tips.

This is why §12 StVO imposes rules on trailers that go significantly beyond the rules for normal vehicles.

---

## Why Trailers Are Not Treated Like Cars

| | Car | Detached Trailer |
|---|---|---|
| Has a driver | Yes | No |
| Will move on its own | Yes | No |
| Purpose on road | Temporary stop | Effectively storage |
| Safety risk if left | Low | Higher — can roll, obstruct sightlines |
| Damages road surface | Less | More (especially heavy trailers) |

A car parked for weeks would also attract attention — but a trailer makes the problem significantly worse because it is designed to be left behind and has no means of moving itself.

---

## Rule 1 — General Parking Rules for Detached Trailers

These rules apply to **all trailers** that are not attached to a towing vehicle.

### Inside Built-Up Areas (innerorts)

A detached trailer may park on a public road for a maximum of **2 weeks**. After 2 weeks, the trailer must be moved — regardless of its size or mass.

### Outside Built-Up Areas (außerorts)

A detached trailer **may not park at all** on public roads outside built-up areas. There is no grace period. Even a brief stop to uncouple a trailer is restricted — the trailer cannot simply be left there.

---

## Rule 2 — Additional Restriction for Trailers Over 2 Tonnes

Trailers with a permissible total mass (*zulässige Gesamtmasse*) exceeding **2 tonnes** face an additional layer of restriction inside built-up areas.

### Restricted Times

These trailers may **not** park in built-up areas during:

- **Sundays and public holidays** — all day
- **Weekdays: between 22:00 and 06:00**

### Where Parking Is Permitted During Restricted Times

During the restricted periods, trailers over 2 tonnes may only park in:

- **Industrial areas** (Industriegebiete)
- **Commercial areas** (Gewerbegebiete)
- **Specially designated parking areas** for heavy vehicles

Residential streets, normal car parks, and general roadside parking in built-up areas are not permitted during these times.

### Why These Times?

These are the times when people are at home — resting, sleeping, or spending weekends with family. Heavy commercial trailers in residential areas during these periods cause noise when manoeuvred or coupled, occupy limited residential parking, and create an environment not suited to the neighbourhood. The rule pushes heavy trailers into commercial and industrial zones where they belong.

---

## Complete Summary

| Trailer | Location | Time | Permitted? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Any detached trailer | Outside built-up area | Any | **No — forbidden** |
| Any detached trailer | Inside built-up area | Any | **Yes — max 2 weeks** |
| Over 2t detached trailer | Inside built-up area | Mon–Sat, 06:00–22:00 | **Yes — max 2 weeks** |
| Over 2t detached trailer | Inside built-up area | Sundays, holidays, or 22:00–06:00 | **Only in industrial/commercial/designated areas** |

---

## Two Questions to Always Ask

When dealing with a trailer parking question, work through these in order:

1. **Is the trailer attached to a towing vehicle?**
   - Yes → normal parking rules apply
   - No → the trailer rules above apply

2. **Does it exceed 2 tonnes permissible total mass?**
   - Yes → also check the day and time before parking in a built-up area
   - No → the 2-week general rule applies without further restriction
